# What broke, and what changed

Real problems found while building and measuring this. Numbers come from `results/`.

1. **Privacy v1 missed half the names.** Presidio with the small spaCy model removed 15 of 30 injected names and 23 of 30 phone numbers (`results/pii_v1.json`). Names at the start of a spoken sentence ("Tomasz said ...") were the worst case. Presidio's phone check also rejects numbers with area codes it cannot validate, but a number a person says out loud is private either way. **v2:** medium spaCy model + a plain phone pattern → 24/30 names, 30/30 phones, 30/30 emails. The v2 changes were chosen by looking at v1's misses on the same synthetic set, so the real-meeting scan below is the out-of-sample check.
2. **Names still leak in real meetings.** Participants say their names in introductions. The speech recognizer heard one of 15 checked names in 12 captures; 2 stored captures still contain one ("Agnes", and "Amina", which the human transcript spells "Alima"). The leak list is hand-made from the human transcripts, so it can miss names nobody checked. Next: a contact list on the device (the wearer's own contacts are the names that matter) and a second pass on first-person introductions ("my name is ...").
3. **Cheap extraction loses recall, and routing does not get it back.** On the Whisper transcripts, routed (Haiku → validator → Sonnet retry → Opus merge) found 19 of 28 human action items; Opus everywhere found 23 of 28. Haiku alone also found 19 there, so the missing 4 were never extracted, and the Opus merge only removed noise (44 → 29 predictions, precision 50% → 69%). On the human transcripts the merge did hurt: Haiku alone found 22, routed 19. Routing is 67% cheaper ($0.135 vs $0.409 for 8 meetings), and that saving costs recall. Next: send windows that contain role-assignment talk ("you'll be", "your job is", "next meeting") to the stronger model, and tell the merge prompt to keep anything with an owner.
4. **The Opus merge is most of the routed cost.** 8 merge calls ≈ $0.10 of $0.135. The merge input is small; effort `medium` on Opus is what costs. Next: try Sonnet for the merge and measure.
5. **Embeddings did not beat keywords.** On 20 frozen questions, BM25 and vector search both put the right capture in the top 3 for 18/20; the hybrid got 17/20 but ranks the right capture first more often (MRR 0.841 vs 0.792 BM25, 0.725 vector). Meeting speech repeats distinctive words ("whistle", "lithium", "Melbourne"), which suits keyword search.
6. **Speech errors cost commitments and numbers.** Haiku finds 22/28 action items on the human transcript and 19/28 on the Whisper transcript. Separately, "twelve fifty Euro" (12.50) came out as "1250", and the summary repeated "production costs capped at 1250 euro per remote" (`results/demo_summary_ES2010a.json`). WER is 22.8% on far-field meeting audio with the `base.en` model. Next: a number-normalization check that flags amounts that disagree with the rest of the meeting, or a larger model for captures that contain numbers.
7. **Whisper sometimes re-decodes a capture.** p95 transcription time is 7.7 s against a 2.4 s median, from temperature fallback on noisy captures. int8 weights are 1.26x faster on average than float32 but have a worse p95 on the 30-clip test.
